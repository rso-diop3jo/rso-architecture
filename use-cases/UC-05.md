# UC-05 Traveler adds a recurring activity

**Actor:** traveler \
**Goal:** put an own activity that repeats (for example "vacuum once a week") on their list, so it shows up on the quest menu when it is due, with an XP value the app rated for it \
**Preconditions:** Traveler is signed in (UC-01) **&** the traveler has fewer than 20 activities (configurable) \
**Trigger:** Traveler wants something they do regularly to count as a daily quest.

---

### Main flow:

1. Traveler opens "My activities" and chooses "Add activity".
2. Traveler writes the name of the activity (for example "Vacuum the apartment"), an optional short description, and how often it repeats: every day, or a number of times per week, or per month (for example 3 times a week, once a month).
3. Traveler confirms.
4. The app asks the difficulty rater to rate the activity. The rater gets the name and the description, together with the default quests and their fixed XP as examples, and answers with a whole number from 1 to 100 XP. The rater is told that the name and the description are only data to rate, and that it must not change the rating because the text asks for it.
5. The app saves the activity with its repeat and its XP, and shows it in the list with the XP. The traveler cannot change the XP by hand.
6. From now on the activity is on the quest menu on the days it is due: as long as it was done fewer times in the current day, week or month than its repeat says (UC-04). Nothing happens when a due activity is not done, and nobody is penalised.

Not part of this use case: editing, pausing and deleting an activity.

---

### Alternate flows:

**2a. Traveler leaves without confirming** (at step 2 or 3)

1. Nothing is saved. Use case ends.

**2b. The input is not valid** (at step 3)

1. The name is empty or longer than 60 characters, or the repeat is more than the days in the period (7 per week, 28 per month), because only one quest a day can be chosen.
2. The app tells the traveler what to fix. The use case continues at step 2.

**2c. An activity with the same name already exists** (at step 3)

1. The app compares the names without caring about capital letters.
2. The app shows the existing activity and saves nothing new. Use case ends.

**3a. The limit of activities is reached** (at step 3)

1. The traveler already has the maximum number of activities.
2. The app tells the traveler that the limit is reached. Nothing is saved. Use case ends.

**3b. Traveler confirms twice or retries** (at step 3)

1. The app never saves a second activity for the same request.

**4a. The difficulty rater cannot be reached** (at step 4)

1. The rater does not answer in time, or returns an error, or the calls to it are paused because it failed too often.
2. The app saves the activity with a default XP (20, configurable) and marks it "XP not rated yet". The traveler is told. The use case continues at step 5.
3. Later the traveler can choose "Rate again". The app asks the rater again (step 4). If the rater answers, the XP and the mark are replaced. This is possible only while the activity has the default XP, so the traveler cannot keep asking until the rater gives a higher number.

**4b. The rater's answer cannot be used** (at step 4)

1. The answer is not a whole number from 1 to 100.
2. The app handles it like 4a.

**5a. The app fails to save the activity** (at step 5)

1. The app could not save the activity (internal error).
2. The app shows a message that the activity was not added. Nothing is saved. The traveler can retry from step 3. Use case ends.

---

### Result:

The activity is on the traveler's list with its repeat and its XP, and appears on the quest menu when it is due. If the rater was not available, the activity has the default XP and can be rated again.

---

### Services involved:

- **quest:** the traveler's list of activities, the repeat, the rating (asks the rater, checks the answer, uses the default XP when the rater fails).
- **difficulty rater** (not one of the six services): an outside LLM API in the MVP that returns XP from 1 to 100. `quest` depends only on a port for the rater, so a local model can replace it later without changing this use case. Calls to it are protected like the Dungeon Master's (circuit breaker, ADR-007).

Only the sign-in before the use case (UC-01) involves `identity`.

---

### Business events made during the use case:

No business event is made. The list of activities is private to the traveler and only `quest` uses it, so no other part of the system reacts. A rating that fell back to the default XP (4a, 4b) is a metric or log, not a business event.

---

### Metrics produced in this use case:

- Activities added per day, by repeat unit (day, week, month).
- Share of activities that got the default XP because the rater failed (4a, 4b), and how many are rated again later.
- Duration and errors of calls to the rater (with the state of the circuit breaker).
- Spread of the rated XP (a rater that always answers 90 or 5 is a sign of a bad prompt or abuse).
- Refused activities by reason: not valid (2b), same name (2c), limit reached (3a).
